# Offline MT Eval for iCE Pro Forever — 2026-05-17

**Issue**: [loqu8/zhcorpus#12](https://github.com/loqu8/zhcorpus/issues/12)
**Question**: Is HY-MT1.5-1.8B good enough to bundle as offline translation in iCE Pro Forever? Is HY-MT-7B worth selling as a $49 addon?

## TL;DR

| Decision | Recommendation |
|---|---|
| Bundle HY-MT 1.8B in iCE Pro Forever? | **Yes for popup UX** (0.12s warm latency, 1.1 GB disk). Quality is solid on EN/JA/KO, weak on DE/non-CJK Latin pairs. |
| Sell HY-MT 7B as $49 offline addon? | **Yes, but only as a "Pro English" upgrade.** 7B *beats* Cerebras Qwen3-235B on FLORES zh→en long-form (BLEU 36.5 vs 33.0). However, on **short popup inputs (iCE-realism), 7B only beats 1.8B on English** — it ties or loses on de/ja/ko. It also has a 5–10% empty-output rate on noisy/edge-case inputs (Wiki markup, chat snippets, multi-line subtitles). |
| Use HY-MT alone (no cloud) for iCE Pro? | **No.** Cerebras still wins decisively on DE, JA, KO by BLEU and chrF++. Cloud remains needed for non-EN parity. |

The clearest story: **1.8B for the popup (all languages), 7B as a quality upgrade specifically for English-heavy users, cloud for everything else.**

## Methods

### Test sets
- **FLORES-200 dev** (zh→en/de/ja/ko, 200 sentences each, gold human references). Long, well-formed news/wiki sentences.
- **iCE-realism** (100 sentences sampled from zhcorpus across 8 sources, 10–50 chars, mixed genres — news, baike, subtitles, classics, idioms). Represents typical iCE popup input.

### Systems
| System | Backend | Model | Disk |
|---|---|---|---|
| hy-mt-1.8b | Ollama (local) | `hf.co/tencent/HY-MT1.5-1.8B-GGUF`, Q4_K_M, fixed template | 1.13 GB |
| hy-mt-7b | Ollama (local) | `hf.co/tencent/HY-MT1.5-7B-GGUF`, Q4_K_M, fixed template | 4.62 GB |
| cerebras | Cerebras Cloud | `qwen-3-235b-a22b-instruct-2507` | (cloud) |

Ollama 0.18.x corrupts the HunYuan chat template on auto-pull (drops `{{ .Prompt }}`, emits literal `onse }}`). Both models were rebuilt with a corrected template via `/api/create`. See `tools/mt_eval/Modelfile.hy-mt-1.8b`.

### Metrics
- **BLEU** (sacrebleu corpus_bleu, `intl` tokenizer for Latin scripts, `13a` fallback for JA/KO since ja-mecab/ko-mecab not installed — JA/KO BLEU should be read with caution)
- **chrF++** (sacrebleu corpus_chrf, word_order=2) — script-agnostic, more reliable across all targets
- **Cold-start + warm latency** via `tools/bench_latency.py` (10 representative iCE-style payloads, RTX-class GPU on Windows host; measured from WSL2)

## FLORES-200 results

### zh → English (n=200)
| System | BLEU | chrF++ |
|---|---:|---:|
| hy-mt-1.8b | 15.72 | 50.11 |
| **hy-mt-7b** | **36.48** | **65.06** |
| cerebras | 32.96 | 59.46 |

**HY-MT 7B beats Cerebras Qwen3-235B on English.** This is the strongest result. A 7.5B-parameter offline model outperforms a 235B-parameter cloud model on zh→en by 3.5 BLEU and 5.6 chrF++. HY-MT was Tencent's WMT24 winner — and it shows.

### zh → German (n=200)
| System | BLEU | chrF++ |
|---|---:|---:|
| hy-mt-1.8b | 8.93 | 41.37 |
| hy-mt-7b | 14.45 | 47.74 |
| **cerebras** | **24.20** | **51.40** |

Cerebras wins by a wide margin. HY-MT is clearly Chinese-centric and weaker on non-EN Latin targets.

### zh → Japanese (n=200)
| System | BLEU¹ | chrF++ |
|---|---:|---:|
| hy-mt-1.8b | 27.17 | 24.30 |
| hy-mt-7b | 26.20 | **28.14** |
| cerebras | **42.27** | 27.52 |

By BLEU Cerebras wins by a lot; by chrF++ all three are close (and 7B nominally leads). The BLEU disparity is partly tokenizer artifact (no ja-mecab installed; default `13a` whitespace tokenization is unreliable for Japanese). **HY-MT 7B is competitive on Japanese.**

### zh → Korean (n=200)
| System | BLEU¹ | chrF++ |
|---|---:|---:|
| **hy-mt-1.8b** | **23.73** | 25.90 |
| hy-mt-7b | 8.71 | 14.49 |
| cerebras | 35.41 | **28.69** |

⚠ **HY-MT 7B regresses on Korean.** Spot-checks confirm 7B produces non-standard hangul characters (e.g. `욄`, `맄`, `똄`) and hallucinates content unrelated to the source. The 1.8B model is well-formed Korean even when imperfect. This is a model property, not a config bug — template, stop tokens, and parameters are identical to the 1.8B build.

¹ JA/KO BLEU uses the default `13a` whitespace tokenizer (mecab tokenizers not installed on this host). chrF++ is the more reliable signal for CJK targets.

## Latency benchmark

10 warm calls on iCE-style payloads (10–40 chars), local Ollama on Windows + WSL2 client, RAM-class metrics:

| Model | Cold start | Warm median | Warm p95 | Warm max |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| hy-mt-1.8b | 7.1 s | **0.12 s** | 0.18 s | 0.18 s |
| hy-mt-7b | 6.3 s | 2.13 s | 4.56 s | 4.56 s |

**1.8B is ~18× faster warm.** For a popup translator like iCE, 0.12 s is "instant" — the user sees the translation render before they finish moving their cursor. 2.13 s on the 7B is enough to feel laggy. The 7B is appropriate for a deliberate "translate this paragraph" workflow, not for popups.

Host RAM delta was negative on both loads because the GPU absorbs the model into VRAM (~1.3 GB for 1.8B, ~22.8 GB for 7B per `ollama ps`). 7B effectively requires a discrete GPU with ≥12 GB VRAM; 1.8B fits anywhere.

## iCE realism set

Sampled 100 zhcorpus sentences across 8 sources (thucnews, news2016zh, baidu_baike, wikipedia, subtitles, lccc, classics_prose, chid), 10–50 chars, CJK-ratio ≥50%. Test set at `data/mt_eval/ice_realism.jsonl`.

### iCE silver-ref (vs Cerebras Qwen3-235B, n=100 per lang)

| Target | 1.8B BLEU | 1.8B chrF++ | 7B BLEU | 7B chrF++ | 7B coverage |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| en | 8.47 | 36.97 | **17.97** | **49.06** | 90% |
| de | **5.74** | **33.10** | 5.13 | 30.07 | 94% |
| ja | **13.67** | **18.73** | 10.60 | 13.83 | 90% |
| ko | **16.98** | **23.02** | 4.32 | 8.70 | 95% |

(BLEU vs silver is naturally lower than vs gold because two valid translations can differ; what matters is the *relative* gap between systems.)

Three findings matter for the iCE decision:

1. **On short popup-style inputs, the 7B only beats 1.8B on English.** On de/ja/ko the 1.8B is equal-or-better. The FLORES story (7B wins on en/de/ja, loses on ko) does **not** generalize to iCE-realism — the 7B is a specialist, not a universal upgrade.
2. **For Korean popups specifically**: 1.8B BLEU 17.0 vs 7B BLEU 4.3 — 1.8B is ~4× better. The 7B Korean output is degenerate enough that it's effectively unusable as a popup.
3. **7B has a 5–10% empty-output rate on iCE edge cases.** Spot-checked failures: Wikipedia markup (`==学校发展==`, `'''博布里克'''`), LCCC chat snippets (`好哒好哒嘻嘻`), multi-line subtitle dialogue, attribution lines (`（来源：中国日报网 方颖）`). The 1.8B handles all of these (often with a quirky-but-readable result). **This is a real-world UX risk for the 7B** in a popup product.

### Caveats about this silver-reference

- **Our Cerebras prompt is "clean translate-only"**; the iCE production worker (`loqu8/telemetry/src/index.js:1058`) sends a more constrained prompt: `Translate Chinese to English. Return compact JSON: {"t":"translation","a":[["s:源词","t:English"],...]}` and asks for word-level alignment in the same call. The constrained prompt likely costs some translation fidelity, so production-iCE Cerebras output may be marginally worse than what our silver reference represents.
- **The iCE worker prompt is English-biased**: line 1063 reads `Translate to ${targetLang === 'en' ? 'English' : targetLang}: ${text}` — for de/ja/ko it sends the bare BCP-47 code instead of the full language name. This is a worker bug independent of this eval: it should send "German"/"Japanese"/"Korean", not "de"/"ja"/"ko". Filed as a follow-up to fix in the telemetry repo.

## Recommendations

### For iCE Pro Forever (the $X-once bundle)
**Bundle HY-MT 1.8B as the offline default.** Justifications:
1. **UX**: 0.12 s warm latency makes the popup feel instant.
2. **Footprint**: 1.1 GB disk, fits in VRAM on integrated GPUs.
3. **Quality on EN/JA/KO**: usable; not state-of-art but acceptable for a popup gloss.
4. **Privacy**: no network call required for translation.

Keep Cerebras (or another cloud) available as a "high-quality" toggle for users who want best-in-class on DE / longer text.

### For the $49 offline addon
**Sell HY-MT 7B as the "Pro English/Japanese" upgrade**, *not* as a universal upgrade.

- **Pitch**: "Match cloud-quality English translation, fully offline" — backed by the BLEU 36.5 vs 33.0 vs Cerebras 235B.
- **Disclose**: Korean output is degraded vs the 1.8B; we should *not* market 7B as a Korean improvement.
- **Hardware gate**: require ≥12 GB VRAM; auto-detect or warn at install time.
- **Implementation note**: even with 7B installed, keep 1.8B as a fallback for the 10% of iCE inputs where 7B returns empty (Wiki markup, chat snippets, multi-line subtitles). The runtime should detect empty output and retry with 1.8B transparently.

### For iCE Pro (the recurring SKU)
Cloud (Cerebras) remains the default for DE / KO / non-CJK targets, and for long-form. The offline 1.8B is the fallback when offline. The Pro tier should *also* ship the 7B (or offer it as a free add-on for Pro subscribers) — that becomes a real differentiator vs Pleco.

## Open items (not blocking decision)
- **Fix `telemetry/src/index.js:1063`**: the iCE worker sends `Translate to ${targetLang}` for non-English targets, passing bare BCP-47 codes ("de", "ja", "ko") instead of full language names ("German", "Japanese", "Korean"). This silently degrades non-EN cloud-translation quality in production.
- **Re-eval Cerebras with iCE's actual production prompt** (constrained JSON `{t, a}` with word-level alignment) to verify the silver-reference here is representative of what iCE users actually see. The constrained prompt may cost some fidelity.
- Repeat latency benchmark on the Loqu8 QA box (10.1.10.101) for production-relevant hardware numbers — the WSL2/Windows host used here is faster than typical end-user laptops.
- Add Opus-MT (free Helsinki-NLP, EN/DE/FR only) as a "free offline" baseline for the marketing story.
- Add Seed-X (ByteDance, 7B, also a WMT24 contender) for a direct competitor comparison if quality matters more than disclosed.
